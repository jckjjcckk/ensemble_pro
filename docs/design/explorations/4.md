# Exploration 4: Bound

Route prefix `/4`. Code in [web/src/explorations/4/](../../../web/src/explorations/4/). Visitor mode: **Operate** on every screen.

Scene: a conservatory studio or a chamber rehearsal in daylight, an iPad on a stand at arm's length. The tool should feel like a bound full score that knows which part you are reading. Light by default; the dim variant is the same room with the lights down, not a dark theme by habit.

## Direction contract

- **THESIS.** The part is the unit of the product, so the part switcher is the score's own index tabs: tabs sit on the top edge of the page, the active one lifted and joined to the paper. It refuses the category default of a dropdown in a toolbar and a sidebar of parts.
- **OWN-WORLD.** Ecru ground, a lighter ecru leaf, graphite ink, one oxblood used only for cuts and failures. Selection is drawn in ink, never in a colour. Old-style serif for titles and every large numeral, a print grotesk for labels. Index tabs, facing leaves with a soft gutter shadow at the binding, folios in the outer corner.
- **STORY.** A player opens the Beethoven and sees Violin I raised among flat tabs, who is on which part and at which bar, and the leader's cut hatched in oxblood. They switch parts with one tap on the edge, and practise the same part with the tabs turned into Solo, With quartet, Click.
- **FIRST VIEWPORT.** Score route at 1194×834: top bar (56px), title row with live presence and four tools (60px), five tabs standing on two facing A4 leaves of about 416×588px each, centred, with page-turn targets in the margins. The footer carries Markings, the pager, and Go to bar. No primary button; the action is reading.
- **FORM.** Single top bar plus the work. No side rails. No seed key: the launcher is absent and the direction was pinned by the README, so `concept-seed` never ran.
- **FINISH.** unreviewed and undocumented is unfinished; this build ends with the finish review, the verdict, DESIGN.md, and every shipping raster carrying its provenance

The FINISH line is not discharged in this run. The README forbids a browser, servers, and files outside this folder, so the finish review, the screenshots and DESIGN.md wait for the owner's side-by-side round. No rasters ship; every image is inline SVG drawn in code.

Where oxblood appears, and nowhere else: the cut hatch, bracket and label on the page; cut entries in the markings list; the "Cut these bars" warning in the composer; Brahms "Reading failed" with its reason and its failed pipeline stage; the engraving error message; the input error on Go to bar and the composer range; listening "lost". Warnings (blurred page, edge cut off) are deliberately **ink**, not oxblood: a warning is not yet a failure.

Notation ink is restyled with CSS on the cloned SVG (`fill` and `stroke` on paths, rects, text, groups), so the dim variant prints ecru notes on umber paper without touching the shared wrapper.

## Type

| Role | Face | Source | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Titles, transcripts, numerals | **EB Garamond** 400/500/600, italic 400/500 | Google Fonts. Georg Duffner and Octavio Pardo, after Garamont's type in the 1592 Egenolff-Berner specimen | Score titles, group names, the wordmark, voice-note transcripts in italic, bar numbers, folios, tempo, loop ends, page labels. Always `oldstyle-nums`; `tabular-nums` where digits change in place (tempo, loop, recording timer) |
| Labels and controls | **Schibsted Grotesk** 400/500/600/700 | Google Fonts. Bakken & Bæck for Schibsted | Navigation, tabs, buttons, metadata, tables (`lining-nums tabular-nums`) |

Loaded with a `<link>` that the root component adds on mount and removes on unmount. Fallbacks are Iowan Old Style / Palatino and the system sans. Neither face is on the skill's list of reflex defaults. Garamond belongs to the printed-score world (the scale of a title page, numerals that sit quietly in a line); the grotesk keeps labels legible at 60 cm. Sizes come from a fixed scale: 12 meta, 13 labels, 14 to 15 controls, 16 body, 18 to 22 serif headings, 26 to 34 serif bar numbers, 48 to 56 serif tempo and current bar.

## Density rules

- **Medium.** One working surface per screen, chrome in three horizontal bands at most (top bar, a title row, a footer or control surface). No side rails, no cards, no nested containers.
- The score gets every pixel left after the bands. Leaf size comes from the stage height at A4 ratio: two leaves when both fit side by side, otherwise one, never cropped.
- Things on the page stay small and sit outside the staff where possible: pins above the bar, presence initials at the bar's top right, trouble underlines below the system, the cut drawn as a hatch over the bars with its label above.
- Every touch target is at least 44px (tabs have a 50px hit area even when the drawn tab is 30px). Practice controls are 54 to 78px.
- Lists carry one fact per column. Library rows have five columns at desk width, two rows of three under 1100px, and stack under 760px.

## Signature move: part tabs, and where they live

| Screen | What the tabs are | Behaviour |
|---|---|---|
| **Document mode** `/4/score/s-beethoven` | Full score, Violin I, Violin II, Viola, Cello, on the top edge of the leaves | One tap re-engraves that part (OSMD hides the other instruments) and keeps your place by reopening at the bar you were reading. Each tab shows who holds the part, where they are or that they are offline, and a count of markings addressed to that part only. **Leader, pianist, librarian:** all tabs raised. **Player:** their own part raised, the others lying flat and quieter, still one tap away. Arrow keys, Home and End move along the tabs. |
| **Practice** `/4/practice` | Solo, With quartet, Click | The same tab shape, with the same lift, now choosing what plays with you. It reads "With orchestra" for an orchestral work and "With quartet" for this one, since the label comes from the ensemble. Click forces the metronome on. Trouble spots sit at the right end of the same tab row when the book is wide enough, and move into the control surface when it is not. |
| **Library** `/4` | Small index tabs standing on each score's rule, one per split part | The same raised and flat logic: the parts you hold or are responsible for are raised. Librarians see who holds each part, or "Unassigned". Every tab carries the part's difficulty. |
| **Scan** `/4/scan` | No tabs. The binding carries the idea instead | Pages appear as facing spreads (1–2, 3–4 …) with a gutter shadow, so the order you commit is visibly the order the score will be bound and turned. |

## Navigation model

- One top bar: placeholder wordmark (`WORDMARK` in `model.ts`, set to "Muse"), the ensemble the current screen belongs to, the four areas (Library, Scan, Score, Practice) as plain links with an ink underline, the role switch, and the light/dim toggle.
- The role switch is a segmented control at 1180px and wider and a native select below that. A role changes what is shown and never where it is: tab raised or flat, tools enabled or disabled (a player cannot cut, so Cut stays in place and is disabled), the brief for the leader, confidence and assignments for the librarian.
- Inside the score: tabs choose the part, ←/→ and PageUp/PageDown turn pages, the margin chevrons and footer pager do the same, Go to bar jumps, tapping a presence face jumps to that member's bar, and Escape backs out of a tool, then the sheet, then the selection.
- Markings, the composer and practice history open in one bottom sheet over the lower part of the stage. It is non-modal and closes with ✕ or Escape. There are no modals anywhere.
- Practice keys: Space plays or pauses, + and − change the tempo by 2, arrows turn pages.

## Screens and states shipped

- **Library.** All six scores grouped by ensemble with online counts. Ready (with bars, meter and tempo, or read confidence for the librarian), processing at 62% with a progress bar, failed with the full reason in oxblood, ordering with "8 pages waiting for a committed order", and the uploaded state shown as the first of four pipeline stages on every unfinished row (Uploaded, Pages ordered, Read and split, Ready), since no score in the data sits in that state. Parts per score with difficulty. Who updated what: the author, kind and bars of the latest marking (filtered to your part when you are a player), or the update time where the data has no author.
- **Scan.** The Fauré draft, eight pages drawn as schematic thumbnails. Photos sit slightly askew on the desk, PDF pages lie flat, page 4 is drawn blurred and page 6 runs off the frame. Drag to reorder with an insertion bar, or use Option and an arrow key. Delete with an undo. Retake clears a page's warning. Both warnings appear on their tiles and in a summary with Retake links. One action, "Commit order and process"; once committed the pages lock and the state says reading has started.
- **Document mode.** Engraved Beethoven via the shared ScoreView. The 227–246 cut is hatched with an oxblood bracket and label on every part. Pins mark the dynamic at 41, the bowing at 57 (Violin I and full score), the viola note at 121 (Viola and full score), the pencil marking at 178 and both voice notes. The markings sheet carries the transcripts in italic serif with a play control. Presence shows four online (Ada, Lin and Inès at 41, Noor at 39) and Tomás offline, in the title row and as initials on the page. Pencil, Note and Voice note: tap a bar, then fill in the sheet. Voice records a timer and saves with "Transcribing". Cut: tap the first bar, then the last, and save; it hatches immediately. Loading, re-engraving and error states draw on the leaf itself.
- **Practice.** Violin I opens on the page holding the loop. Loop 178–190 is tinted and bracketed in ink, with the current bar boxed. Tempo 120 is shown as "of 132 · 91%", the metronome is on, "With quartet" is selected and listening reads "following". Trouble spots are drawn as dashed underlines whose weight follows severity, and listed as one-tap buttons that loop them. History is one tap away: 104 to 120 bpm over four sessions, each session's length, tempo against target, accuracy and loops. The mock transport advances beats at the set tempo, wraps the loop, skips the cut and turns pages.

## How the score is drawn (worth keeping)

`useEngraving` mounts the shared `ScoreView` off screen at a fixed engraving width (680px for a part, 900px for the full score) with `pageFormat: 'A4_P'` and `autoResize: false`. It hides instruments with `Instrument.Visible` plus `updateGraphic()`, then reads every bar's box from `GraphicSheet.MeasureList` as fractions of its page. The visible leaves are clones of OSMD's page SVGs, scaled by CSS, so the two-up layout never feeds back into OSMD's layout, and every overlay is a percentage box that survives any size. A MutationObserver tells it when OSMD has redrawn.

## Assumptions (no interview; decided and recorded)

1. Each role acts as one member: player is Lin Zhou (Violin I), leader Ada Okafor, pianist CK Jiang, librarian Sam Whitfield. The default role is **leader**, so document mode opens on the full score with every required marking reachable. Choosing Player shows the raised-tab view.
2. Only the leader can cut. Players' markings are scoped to their own part.
3. Practice always shows the shared practice state (Violin I) whatever the role, because it is the only practice data.
4. The Brahms failure's "Retake pages" action and the Fauré "Order pages" action both open the scan intake, the only intake screen; the data holds only the Fauré draft.
5. The page counts the app engraves differ from the scanned page counts in the data (`pages`). The library shows the data's counts; the score footer shows the engraved pages.
6. Dates and times are shown in UTC so every device matches the mock.
7. Presence in the score lists the people reading it (the conductor and the four players). The librarian is online but not reading, so is not shown there.
8. Voice playback is a timed mock with no audio. New voice notes save without a transcript and say so.

## Not verified here

Per the README there was no browser and no server. The build passes `tsc -b && vite build` with zero errors, but nothing has been seen on screen. Risks to check first in the owner's screenshot round: OSMD engraving time for the full 313 bars on an iPad (one extra render happens on load when a single part is shown); whether the A4 aspect leaves feel too small at 1194×834 for a part (lower `PART_WIDTH` toward 600 for larger notation per page); collisions between pins and presence initials on narrow bars.

## What I would cut if this became the product

- **The role switch itself.** Role comes from membership. The switch is a design-phase instrument, and in the product it becomes a "view as part" for leaders.
- **Difficulty ticks on library tabs.** They are low-value per part, and the tabs read cleaner with name and holder only.
- **The margin page-turn chevrons.** They duplicate the footer pager and arrow keys. On stands, use tap zones or a pedal.
- **Click as both a tab and a toggle.** Merge them: keep the Solo, With quartet, Click tabs and drop the separate metronome toggle, or the reverse.
- **The leader's brief inline in the library.** It belongs at the top of the score's markings sheet, where it is read before rehearsal.

## Three things the other directions should steal

1. **Tabs that answer "which part, who's on it, how much is said about it".** Whatever form your part switcher takes, put each part's holder, their live bar or offline state, and a count of part-only markings on the switcher itself. The leader sees who is lost without opening a presence panel.
2. **The page-true geometry layer.** Engrave off screen at a fixed width, clone the page SVGs into your own frames, and keep every bar as a fractional box per page. Cuts, loops, trouble spots, pins and presence then become plain absolutely positioned elements that hold at any viewport, in one or two pages, light or dark. `useEngraving`, `segments` and `Book` in `engraving.tsx` lift out unchanged.
3. **Scan order shown as binding order, and failure shown at the stage where it stopped.** Pair scanned pages as facing spreads so a wrong order looks wrong before anything is read. Give every unfinished score the same four-stage pipeline, and draw a failure on the stage that failed rather than as a generic error badge. That is principle 4 (honest machine output) made visible.
