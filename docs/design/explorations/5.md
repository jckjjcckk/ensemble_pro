# 5. Instrument

Practice-first. The other screens exist to get the musician to the music rest with the score open and the right bars looping. Code lives in `web/src/explorations/5/`; routes `/5`, `/5/scan`, `/5/score/s-beethoven` (any ready score works at `/5/score/:id`), `/5/practice`.

## Direction contract

**THESIS.** The score sits above a control surface that the right hand can find without looking: one graphite dock, always at the bottom, carries the three areas and, in practice, grows into the instrument's panel with tempo as the largest number on screen. It refuses the category default of a toolbar over the page and a modal settings sheet for loop and tempo.

**OWN-WORLD.** A graphite dock in both themes (the body of the instrument), score pages that follow ambient light (pale sheet with graphite ink by day, graphite sheet with pale ink at night), one green that means only "following" and the metronome pulse, one amber that means only "lost", warnings and trouble. Bar and tempo readouts set in a condensed monospace because they are measurements; everything else in one grotesk with tabular figures. The signature shape is a bracket: a top bar with two legs, drawn on the ruler, echoed in the score at the loop's ends, and reused as the drop slot when ordering scan pages.

**STORY.** The musician opens the app, sees where they left off (bars 178 to 190, 120 of 132), presses Resume, and lands on the two facing pages that contain the loop, tinted, with the trouble spot shaded under the staff and the leader's voice note pinned at 178. They play; the word next to the bar number says "following". They nudge tempo with the right thumb, drag the bracket a few bars wider, and never leave the score.

**FIRST VIEWPORT (practice, iPad landscape 1194 × 834).** 56 px header: title, part tag "Violin I", History. **FORM.** The pinned brief's direction 5, built as written. No concept-seed roll: the Impeccable launcher is absent and the README pins the direction, so the roll, decision page and comp round do not apply (Operate surfaces are code-led regardless). Seed key: none, direction pinned by brief.

**FINISH.** unreviewed and undocumented is unfinished; this build ends with the finish review, the verdict, DESIGN.md, and every shipping raster carrying its provenance

## Scene and theme

A pianist at a Casio PX-S1100 with the iPad on the music rest in landscape, or a violinist at a stand; daylight practice rooms and late evenings under one lamp. Theme follows `prefers-color-scheme`, so the device's own ambient-light switching drives it. The dock stays graphite in both: it is the instrument, not the page, and it keeps the controls in the same place and contrast whatever the room.

Failure has no red. The brief allows green and amber only; a failed score is amber with a filled warning triangle and the word "Failed", and its reason is printed in full. If testing shows failures and warnings blur together, the fix is a stronger glyph, not a third hue.

## Type

- **Archivo** (Omnibus-Type, Google Fonts, variable `wdth` 62–125, `wght` 100–900) for every label, title and control, with `tnum` on everywhere so counts and percentages align. One family carries the UI; the wordmark uses its wide cut.
- **Martian Mono** (Evil Martians, Google Fonts, variable `wdth` 75–112.5, `wght` 100–800), condensed to `wdth` 75–87.5, only for measurements: the tempo readout (116 px light in the tall dock), the bar readout, ruler scale and loop labels, bar numbers in lists and the tempo chart.
- Loaded with one `<link>` appended on mount (`App.tsx`). Fallbacks are the system UI and monospace stacks.
- Dynamic markings in pins ("sub. p") use an italic system serif, the way dynamics are engraved. That is the only third voice.
- Scale is fixed rem steps (12.5, 13.5, 14.5, 16, 17, 20) for UI; the two readouts are the only display sizes.

## Density

- **Score:** lowest. Two facing pages in landscape, one in portrait; page shapes come from six aspect buckets so the music fills the stage instead of scrolling. Over the ink only: loop tint, one bracket at each end, heat under the staff, the current-bar cursor, compact pins (one line, 26 px) for markings. Full text only in a popover or the side panel.
- **Dock:** highest. Every practice control lives there, at 52 to 76 px targets, plus the ruler with loop, heat, cut and playhead.
- **Library and scan:** medium, list rows rather than cards; one lead strip per role.

## Navigation model

A bottom dock, always present, with exactly three areas: Library, Score, Practice. It sits at the dock's left, a row of three in compact docks and a stacked column in the tall practice dock. Scan intake is reached from the library (dock action "Scan pages", the Fauré row's "Order pages") and highlights Library. Page turns are edge buttons, arrow and Page keys on the focused stage, and in document mode the ruler's view bracket, which can be dragged to scrub through the piece. Side panels (practice history, markings) dock as a column on wide screens (≥ 1360 px) and overlay the right page elsewhere. Practice keys on a Mac: Space play, minus and plus tempo, M metronome, O other parts, L listening, H history.

## Signature move: the loop bracket, per screen

- **Practice.** The ruler above the dock carries the draggable bracket (pointer events, so mouse, touch and pencil; arrow keys on each end as sliders, Shift for 10 bars). The same span is tinted in the score with bracket marks at its ends, trouble heat sits under the ruler and under the staff, and the playhead and cursor turn green while following, amber when lost. Tap a heat band for its note and "Loop these bars".
- **Score (document).** The same ruler shows the cut hatched, markings and voice notes as marks under the line, and where members are; its bracket is the bars currently on screen and moves the pages when dragged. Adding a cut turns it into an editable range bracket and hatches the proposal in the score.
- **Library.** The lead strip for players and pianists shows the last practice position as a read-only ruler (loop, heat, playhead, cut); the leader's strip shows the group on the same ruler.
- **Scan.** The bracket becomes the drop slot when a page is dragged, and the amber heat band marks the two pages with warnings.

## Screens and states

- **Library:** six scores in three libraries; ready (with read confidence), processing 62% with a meter, failed with the reason, ordering, and uploaded represented as an honest zero in the state filter with a teaching empty state. Parts per score, difficulty, last change and who made it. Role switch (Player, Leader, Pianist, Librarian) swaps the lead strip, the part tags (own part raised for players, assignee initials for the librarian), difficulty (own part vs. hardest part) and action labels; positions never move.
- **Scan:** the eight Fauré pages in order, drag to reorder (pointer events with a floating copy and the bracket slot), Option-arrow to move from the keyboard, delete with Undo in the dock, retake (marks the page as waiting for a new photo), both warnings above the grid and on their pages, and one Process action labelled with the count. Processing starts only on Process; the order then locks and the library shows the Fauré as queued.
- **Score:** ScoreView renders the Beethoven; part switcher in the dock (Score, Violin I, Violin II, Viola, Cello) re-engraves the selected part; cut 227–246 hatched on the score and the ruler; dynamic 41, bowing 57, viola note 121, pencil 178 and two voice notes as pins with full text and transcripts in popovers and the Markings panel; presence in the header (four online: three at 41, one at 39; Tomás offline) and as faces at those bars in the score; add a marking, a cut or a voice note from the dock.
- **Practice:** Violin I, loop 178–190, 120 of 132 (91%), metronome on, other parts on, listening following; trouble spots in the score, under the ruler and in History; four sessions with a tempo chart, accuracy and loops.

## Assumptions (no user was interviewed)

- "Who updated what": the latest marking's author when one is newer than the score; otherwise the ensemble's librarian for intake actions and the owner for the personal library. The mock carries no `updatedBy`.
- The single practice state is treated as the current user's for both Player and Pianist roles.
- Presence is shown only on the Beethoven, the score the presence data describes; the librarian is not counted as a reader.
- No score has state `uploaded`; it appears as a zero count with its empty state rather than an invented score.
- Voice-note playback and recording are simulated clocks over the real durations; no audio exists in the mock. New voice notes say their transcript arrives after processing.
- "Other parts" offers On (all parts) or Off for a quartet; the Orchestra option belongs to concerto scores.
- Retake marks the page as waiting; capture itself belongs to the device camera flow.
- The wordmark is the placeholder "Muse" from `index.html`, one constant in `store.tsx`.

## What I would cut if this became the product

- The tall dock on Mac. A desk user does not need 116 px tempo; the Mac could use the one-row dock at every width.
- The bar-tap seek in practice. With real MIDI following, the instrument moves the cursor and taps on the music rest become accidental.
- Presence faces inside the score. The header list and ruler ticks already answer "where is everyone"; faces over the staves are the one place chrome touches the music in document mode.
- The library's per-role lead strip for the leader. The rehearsal strip duplicates the score header.

## Three things the other directions should steal

1. **One ruler, many jobs.** A bar ruler component that draws loop, heat, cuts, marks, presence and a playhead, and whose bracket edits a loop, a cut or the visible pages. It gives every screen the same map of the piece for the cost of one component.
2. **Measure OSMD, then draw beside it.** `geometry.ts` reads `GraphicSheet.MeasureList` and the page SVG boxes after every render (by wrapping the instance's `render`) and returns per-bar rectangles in stage coordinates, so tints, cuts, cursors and pins sit exactly on the bars without touching OSMD's DOM. Page shapes chosen from a few aspect buckets keep two facing pages filling the screen without reloading on every resize.
3. **The listening word.** A single word next to the bar number, green "following" or amber "lost", echoed as the colour of the cursor and the ruler playhead, is the whole listening UI. No meters, no waveform.
